# Lesson 1: Starting currency is a retention lever

## Opening question

### gd-econ/endowment/open · open

Top Eleven is a football manager game by Nordeus. New players started with 40 Tokens, its premium currency. In a [published test](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186407), some new players got 80 or 120 instead. Before reading on, guess: what happened to day-7 retention, and did players buy fewer Tokens with real money?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Retention went up in both bigger groups, and purchases did not fall. Read on for the numbers and the catch.
</details>

## The idea

A faucet aimed at new players is not only a cost to monetization. It can be a retention lever.

Nordeus and Edward Castronova [ran a randomized test](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186407) on about 575,000 new Top Eleven players, split equally into three groups: 40 Tokens (the control, the default since late 2014), 80 and 120. It ran for 28 days, one in-game season. The paper is peer-reviewed (PLOS ONE).

Against control, day-7 retention was 6.9% higher with 80 Tokens and 7.7% higher with 120. Both were significant against control (p = 0.015 and 0.005), but not different from each other. On purchases, the daily share of players making a first real-money purchase rose by about 8% and 4%, but neither was statistically significant (p = 0.054 and 0.197). The size of that first purchase rose 14% (80 Tokens, p = 0.057, just short of significance) and 34.8% (120 Tokens, p = 0.005). All figures are from the [paper's Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186407).

Now the catch. The [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186407) says a bigger money supply "leads to inflation and increased money demand". The paper measured no prices and no inflation; Token packages keep fixed real-money prices. So the inflation part is the authors' reading, not a result. What was measured: more free currency at the start, more players still there a week later, and no drop in buying. The authors also say they cannot locate the best amount, only that it lies somewhere around 80 to 120.

## Why

### gd-econ/endowment/why · why

Why might giving away premium currency at the start raise later purchase size instead of replacing purchases? Write two or three sentences before opening.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>
Players who get more currency early can try more of the paid systems, so they learn what the currency is worth to them and may settle on a higher spending level. Players who stay longer also have more chances to buy, so even unchanged buying per day adds up. This is my explanation (grade C); the paper reports the outcome, not the mechanism, and it says only that whether such reference points carry across games is uncertain.
</details>

## Scenario

### gd-econ/endowment/scenario · scenario

Your live team wants to cut the new-player gem grant in a mid-core strategy game from 500 to 250 "to protect monetization". A product lead counters with this paper and wants to double it instead. You are asked to settle it. What do you say?

<details>
<summary>Explained answer</summary>
Neither side can settle it from this paper. It is one game, one genre, one 28-day window and a range of 40 to 120 Tokens, so it shows that a bigger grant did not hurt early buying in Top Eleven, not that it will in your game. A strategy game's gems may also flow into longer-term sinks, where an early surplus could shorten the time before players hit a paywall or could cheapen it. The useful move is to run the test yourself: randomize new installs into grant sizes, read day-7 retention, conversion and first-purchase size, and keep watching past the first month, because the paper's window ended at 28 days and later effects are unknown.
</details>

## Recall

### gd-econ/endowment/r1 · recall

What were the three starting Token amounts in the Top Eleven test, and which was the control?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
40, 80 and 120 Tokens. 40 was the control.
</details>

### gd-econ/endowment/r2 · recall

Which purchase result was statistically significant?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Only the size of the first purchase in the 120-Token group, up 34.8% against control. The change in how many players converted was not significant in either group.
</details>

### gd-econ/endowment/r3 · recall

Did the paper measure inflation?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
No. It measured retention and purchases. The inflation claim in the abstract is the authors' interpretation.
</details>
